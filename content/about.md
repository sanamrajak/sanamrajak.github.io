I am a passionate materials and surface scientist currently finishing my PhD at [IMTEK](https://www.imtek.uni-freiburg.de/), University of Freiburg. I work at [the Chair of Chemistry and Physics of Interfaces] (https://uni-freiburg.de/tf-imtek-cpi/) under the supervision of Prof. Dr. Jürgen Rühe. As a part of Collaborative Research Center (CRC) 1537, [ECOSENSE](https://uni-freiburg.de/ecosense/), Project C2, I developed self-cleaning surfaces with anti-biofouling and anti-soiling properties that remain clean and functional throughout the vegetation period during outdoor exposure.

My research has focused on fabricating functional thin-film coatings using a range of deposition techniques, including spin coating, dip coating, spray coating, and doctor blading, followed by the implementation of C–H insertion crosslinking processes. Furthermore, I characterized these self-cleaning coatings and evaluated their performance using a variety of advanced techniques, including XPS, SEM, FIB-SEM, TEM, WLI, ellipsometry, AFM, nanotribology, contact angle measurements, and other analytical methods. I enjoy understanding how materials function and how their properties can be tailored to address real-world challenges, which is what makes this field particularly exciting to me.

I completed my M.Sc. in Advanced Functional Materials at TU Chemnitz, Germany, in 2022. During my master's studies, I worked on several research projects. During my second semester, I completed an internship in [the Department of Technical Physics] (https://www.tu-chemnitz.de/physik/TEPH/index.php.en) under the supervision of Dr. Göhler and Prof. Dr. Thomas Seyller. My research focused on the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopic (XPS) analysis of a special class of misfit-layer compounds known as Ferecrystals. Ferecrystals are two-dimensional van der Waals heterostructures characterized by rotational disorder between their constituent layers. I characterized these structures to investigate charge transport between the layers, the electronic band structure, the surface morphology, and rotational disorder using XPS, angle-resolved ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (ARUPS), atomic force microscopy (AFM), and low-energy electron diffraction (LEED).
